## How the encryption works

Before an entry leaves your device, your browser encrypts it: the words become a random-looking string of characters (what our server receives) that can be turned back into text only with one specific key — a long random number made on your device and never sent to us. Your browser holds it as a "non-extractable key," whose raw value no code can read, this app's included.

When you set your passphrase, you also receive a recovery code. Each one encrypts its own copy of that key, on your device, and only those two encrypted copies reach our server — so any of your devices can rebuild the key once you enter your passphrase or recovery code. The passphrase and recovery code never leave your browser. This is the design Signal, Proton Mail, and Bitwarden use. Encryption cannot be turned off.

**Two honest limits every encrypted web app lives with.** First, the app is a website: your browser downloads its code from us on each visit, so you are trusting that code. For us to read your entries we would have to send your browser an altered app that steals your key as you type. That code would run in your own browser, open to anyone who inspects it — not a silent query against a database. Second, encryption hides the contents of your entries, not the fact of them: the server still sees that entries exist, roughly how big each is, and when each last changed.

<Deepdive>
Every entry is encrypted with `AES-256-GCM` — an authenticated cipher, so a tampered entry fails to open rather than decrypting to something wrong — under a fresh random nonce each time. The entry's identifier and timestamps are bound into that check, so a moved, rolled-back, or resurrected entry fails to open too. All of your entries share one random 256-bit key, made on your device.

That key is encrypted twice, so it can travel between your devices without ever being exposed: once under a key stretched from your passphrase with `PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA-256, 600,000 iterations` and a random salt, and once under a key stretched from a ~120-bit recovery code. The server stores only those two encrypted copies, written once and never silently replaced; it can open neither, and they can be removed only by deleting your account.

On your device the key is held as a `non-extractable CryptoKey` — the browser decrypts with it but will not export its bytes, even to the page's own scripts. What rests on the server is the encrypted text of each entry plus the fields sync needs in the clear: an identifier, timestamps, and a change counter. No title, text, or tags.
</Deepdive>

## Voice

If you speak an entry with the microphone, the app streams your audio to a transcription service and the words come back as you speak. We never receive or store the audio — it goes straight from your device to the service. Typing never touches the network.

<Providers />

One honest edge: if none of those services can be reached, the app falls back to your browser's own dictation. That path is the browser's, not ours — on most devices the browser sends the audio to its maker's speech servers (Google's or Apple's), under their policies, and that traffic does not show in this app's network activity.

## Google Drive backup

If you connect Google Drive, unencrypted copies land in a folder in your own Drive named "Snippets" — so that if you lose your passphrase and recovery code you can still import your entries, and if this app ever disappears you still hold an up-to-date copy. These are plain, readable files by design; that is what lets them rescue you. **They live in your Google account, where Google can read them like any other file in your Drive** ([Google's privacy policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy)).

The permission this app uses (`drive.file`) lets it see only files it created; it cannot read, list, or touch anything else in your Drive. Your device makes these files and uploads them straight to Google, so our server never receives them — and it keeps none of your Drive credentials.

## What we collect

Signing in needs your email address — and, if you use Google, the name and profile picture on your Google account. That is all. We use no passwords, and signing in is never required to use the app. The only emails we send are the ones you cause: a sign-in code, or a warning that your backups have stopped going through.

## Leaving

**Erasing your entries** ([Settings](/settings), "Let everything go") marks every entry deleted — on the server and on every signed-in device — so they disappear everywhere at once. The content is then kept for up to 30 days, so a mistaken erase can be undone from another device, after which it is scrubbed to an id and timestamps — no words — which stops the entry reappearing.

**Deleting your account** (Settings → Account) removes the account itself — your sign-in, the Google connection, every synced entry and its deletion-marker, and the backup record — from the server immediately and for good; we also ask Google to revoke the Drive connection. Copies on your devices, and backup files already in your Drive, remain yours.
