The [privacy policy](/privacy) makes claims. This page is how you check them yourself: three tests, a few minutes, no trust required. If any of them ever fails, the claims are false and we want to know: [snippets.support@gmail.com](mailto:snippets.support@gmail.com).

## Test 1: Airplane mode

The claim: while you're signed out, your entries never leave this device; writing needs no network at all.

<Steps>
  <Step n={1}>Open Snippets once, so your browser saves a copy that runs offline.</Step>
  <Step n={2}>Turn on airplane mode.</Step>
  <Step n={3}>Write an entry and press the wax seal. It saves. Look through your entries. Everything works. (Voice is the one exception: transcription needs the network, including your browser's own dictation fallback, so the microphone will not work with the network off.)</Step>
  <Step n={4}>Close the app, reopen it, still offline: your entry is there.</Step>
</Steps>

**There is nothing to disconnect from:** signed out, the app has no server side. Your entries are saved in this device's own storage.

## Test 2: Watch the network

The claim: every request this app makes is one you can predict, and none of them carries readable entries.

<Steps>
  <Step n={1}>On a computer, open the browser's developer tools (usually F12) and pick the Network tab.</Step>
  <Step n={2}>Use Snippets: write, browse, sign in if you like.</Step>
  <Step n={3}>Read the request list against this table:</Step>
</Steps>

<Requests>
  <Req name="snippets.ink">the app itself: pages, scripts, styles, fonts, paper textures.</Req>
  <Req name="/_vercel/insights">anonymous page-view counting. It carries no entry data; open one and look.</Req>
  <Req name="/api/auth/…">signing in and the session check. Only if you use an account.</Req>
  <Req name="/api/sync/push, /api/sync/pull">your entries to and from the server, encrypted. **Open a push request and read what it sends: a block of random-looking characters, not words.** That block is exactly what our server stores.</Req>
  <Req name="/api/keys">the two encrypted copies of your key (made when you set your passphrase). The passphrase itself never appears; search the request for it.</Req>
  <Req name="/api/…-token">a temporary pass for the transcription service, fetched just before the microphone opens. The permanent key never leaves our server.</Req>
  <ProviderRow />
  <Req name="www.googleapis.com">backup files traveling from this device to your own Google Drive. Only if you connected the backup.</Req>
</Requests>

One honest gap: if the app ever falls back to your browser's built-in dictation, that audio is sent by the browser itself (to Google's or Apple's speech servers) and does not show in this tab. The privacy policy's Voice section covers it.

## Test 3: The list your browser enforces

The claim: this app **cannot** send data anywhere you haven't seen above, not by policy, but because your browser refuses to let it.

Every page we serve carries a `Content-Security-Policy` header. Its `connect-src` line is the list from Test 2, and your browser blocks any address not on it before the request can leave. Read it yourself from any terminal:

```
curl -sI https://www.snippets.ink | grep -i content-security
```

That list is the whole of where this app can send anything. It is your browser enforcing it, not us asking you to believe it.

**What the three tests give you.** Together they let you confirm, with your own eyes, that the app works offline, that everything it sends is encrypted, and that your browser itself blocks any address off the list, whatever the code intends. You are not trusting our word for it, and because a website loads its code fresh each visit, it is a check you can run again anytime.

Want to go deeper? The privacy policy's [“How the encryption works”](/privacy#how-the-encryption-works) section names the exact algorithms and what rests on the server.
